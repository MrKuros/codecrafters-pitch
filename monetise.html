Why system design interviews are broken

Current system design interviews produce noisy signal. The score is heavily correlated with how well a candidate talks about architecture — not whether they can produce it. A candidate who has read the right books can sound like a staff engineer. A candidate who has actually scaled production systems but communicates differently fails.

The whiteboard has no test runner. There is no ground truth. Two interviewers in the same session frequently disagree on the same candidate. Companies know this is broken. They have no alternative.

What the B2B product looks like

Step 01
Send

Company sends candidate a challenge link. Senior, staff, or principal tier.

Step 02
Build

Candidate works locally for 2–3 hours. Their own machine, their own tools.

Step 03
Push

Candidate pushes when done. Test runner grades it automatically.

Step 04
Report

Company receives structured report: stages passed, latency numbers, solutions.

Why nobody else can build this

HackerRank can't do this. HackerRank's entire infrastructure is browser-based sandboxed execution. It cannot run Docker Compose. It cannot inject real network failures. It cannot test actual distributed behaviour across containers. The browser sandbox produces toy signal. Constraints produces production signal.

The local execution model is the moat. The fact that Constraints runs on the candidate's own machine — their terminal, their Docker, their tools — is what makes the signal real. It also makes it structurally impossible for a browser-based competitor to replicate without rebuilding their entire infrastructure.

CodeCrafters already has the content. The challenges exist. The test runner model is proven. The B2B product is the same content licensed differently. The marginal cost of adding company assessments is low.
